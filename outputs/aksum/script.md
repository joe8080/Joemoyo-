# Aksum: The African Empire That Outlasted Rome — Script & Beat Sheet

**Runtime target:** 12–13 min · **Word count:** ~2,000 · **Voice:** deep documentary narrator
**Legend:** `[STILL]` = AI image + Ken Burns · `[CLIP]` = rationed AI hero video (6–10 total) · `[CARD]` = motion graphic

> Each beat = one line of VO + one visual. Visual prompts already carry the OrigineX art-direction string
> ("cinematic, dramatic volumetric lighting, historically accurate, rich earth-and-gold palette, film grain,
> shallow depth of field, epic scale, museum-documentary realism").

---

### COLD OPEN (0:00–0:20) — hook before the title

**B1 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "In the third century, a Persian prophet listed the four greatest powers on Earth. Rome. Persia. China." — *Visual:* slow push over an ancient world map lit by candlelight, three empires glowing.

**B2 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "And a fourth — a kingdom in Africa that most history classes have never once named." — *Visual:* the fourth light ignites over the Horn of Africa, gold bloom.

**B3 [STILL]** — *VO:* "It minted its own gold coins while much of Europe still bartered. It adopted a world religion on its own terms. And when Rome fell in the West, this empire was still standing." — *Visual:* an Aksumite gold coin spinning, catching light.

**B4 [STILL]** — *VO:* "This is the story of Aksum — and why you were never taught it." — *Visual:* colossal granite stele silhouetted against a blood-orange dawn.

**B5 [CARD]** — Title card: **AKSUM — The African Empire That Outlasted Rome** (gold-on-dark, heavy condensed serif).

---

### ACT I — A power on the Red Sea (0:20–3:00)

**B6 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Rise with me above the Horn of Africa, in what is now Ethiopia and Eritrea." — *Visual:* aerial sweep over highland plateaus and escarpments at dawn.

**B7 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Here, around two thousand years ago, a city grew rich on a simple accident of geography." — *Visual:* a highland city of stone taking shape in a green valley.

**B8 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Aksum sat at the hinge of the ancient world's trade — between the Roman Mediterranean and the markets of India and Arabia." — *Visual:* trade routes drawn in gold across a Red Sea map.

**B9 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "Its gateway was the port of Adulis, on the Red Sea coast — one of the busiest harbors of the ancient world." — *Visual:* a bustling ancient port, dhows and Roman ships, cranes of ivory and cloth.

**B10 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Out of Africa flowed ivory, gold, emeralds, incense, and exotic animals." — *Visual:* a caravan of goods, elephant tusks stacked, glittering emeralds.

**B11 [STILL]** — *VO:* "In came silver, wine, olive oil, and fine cloth from across the sea." — *Visual:* Roman amphorae and bolts of dyed cloth unloaded at a quay.

**B12 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Aksum didn't just trade at the edge of empires. It became one." — *Visual:* a king enthroned, advisors around him, banners.

**B13 [STILL]** — *VO:* "At its height, Aksumite power reached across the Red Sea into southern Arabia — an African kingdom holding territory on two continents." — *Visual:* map with Aksum's reach spanning into Yemen/Himyar.

**B14 [CARD]** — Stat card: **4** — "One of only four powers the ancient world ranked as truly great" (count-up animation).

---

### ACT II — The empire that made its own money (3:00–5:30)

**B15 [STILL]** — *VO:* "There is one thing an empire does that a mere town never can. It mints coins." — *Visual:* a mint, molten metal poured into coin dies.

**B16 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "From around the year 270, Aksum struck its own currency — in gold, silver, and bronze." — *Visual:* three coins tumbling in slow motion, catching firelight.

**B17 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Only a handful of ancient states ever did this. Rome. Persia. And Aksum — the only sub-Saharan African power to mint gold in antiquity." — *Visual:* three coins side by side on dark velvet, labels in gold.

**B18 [STILL]** — *VO:* "The coins carried the king's face, and words in Greek — the language of international trade — so merchants from Alexandria to India would honor them." — *Visual:* close macro of Greek lettering around a royal portrait.

**B19 [STILL]** — *VO:* "This was soft power in metal. A promise, backed by an African state, that traveled farther than any army." — *Visual:* a coin passing hand to hand across a trading floor.

**B20 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Aksumite coins have been dug up as far away as India and the Arabian Peninsula — proof of a reach we're only now measuring." — *Visual:* archaeologist's gloved hand lifting a coin from Indian soil.

**B21 [CARD]** — Card: "GOLD · SILVER · BRONZE — a full imperial coinage" (three pills animate in).

---

### ACT III — The stones that touched the sky (5:30–8:00)

**B22 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "But to see how ambitious Aksum truly was, you have to stand beneath its stones." — *Visual:* camera cranes up the face of a towering granite stele.

**B23 [STILL]** — *VO:* "The Aksumites raised giant carved obelisks — single pieces of granite, quarried, moved, and stood upright." — *Visual:* the stelae field at dawn, long shadows.

**B24 [STILL]** — *VO:* "The tallest that still stands rises over twenty meters. Carved to look like a multi-story building — false doors, false windows, floor upon floor reaching for the sky." — *Visual:* detail of the carved 'storeys' and false door on a stele.

**B25 [STILL]** — *VO:* "One was even larger — over thirty meters, the largest single block of stone humans ever tried to raise anywhere on Earth." — *Visual:* reconstruction of the great fallen stele upright, dwarfing people.

**B26 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "It broke and fell — perhaps during the attempt. But think of the confidence it took to try at all." — *Visual:* dramatic slow-motion of the colossal stele toppling, dust exploding.

**B27 [STILL]** — *VO:* "These weren't just monuments. They were grave markers for kings — engineering and faith, fused in stone." — *Visual:* underground royal tomb chambers beneath the stelae.

**B28 [STILL]** — *VO:* "And in the twentieth century, one of them was stolen." — *Visual:* a stele being lowered, 1930s trucks and soldiers.

**B29 [STILL]** — *VO:* "In 1937, Fascist Italy looted a 24-meter obelisk and shipped it to Rome as a war trophy." — *Visual:* the obelisk re-erected on a Roman street, period photo styling.

**B30 [STILL]** — *VO:* "It stood in Rome for nearly seventy years. Ethiopia never stopped asking for it back." — *Visual:* protesters and diplomats, decades passing.

**B31 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "In 2008, it finally came home — flown back in pieces and raised again on Aksumite soil." — *Visual:* the obelisk rising once more at Aksum, crowds cheering, gold light.

**B32 [CARD]** — Stat card: **33m** — "The largest single stone humanity ever tried to raise" (count-up).

---

### ACT IV — A crossroads of faith (8:00–10:00)

**B33 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Around the year 330, Aksum did something that would echo for seventeen centuries." — *Visual:* a king in contemplation in a stone hall, light through a high window.

**B34 [STILL]** — *VO:* "King Ezana converted to Christianity — making Aksum one of the very first empires on Earth to adopt it as a state faith." — *Visual:* Ezana receiving a cross, early Christian iconography, Aksumite style.

**B35 [STILL]** — *VO:* "You can watch it happen in the coins themselves. The old crescent-and-disc symbol vanishes — and a cross takes its place." — *Visual:* two coins side by side: pagan symbol → cross.

**B36 [STILL]** — *VO:* "That makes Aksumite money some of the earliest coinage in the world to carry the Christian cross." — *Visual:* macro of the cross struck into gold.

**B37 [STILL]** — *VO:* "This was not a faith imposed from outside. Aksum took it, made it its own, and built a Christian tradition that survives in Ethiopia to this day." — *Visual:* a rock-hewn Ethiopian church, priests in white.

**B38 [STILL]** — *VO:* "And it is here that one of history's greatest legends took root." — *Visual:* candlelit sanctuary, a guarded chapel.

**B39 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "Aksum is where tradition says the Ark of the Covenant was brought — and where, believers say, it still rests, guarded, in a chapel no visitor may enter." — *Visual:* slow push toward the veiled chapel of the Ark, incense drifting.

**B40 [STILL]** — *VO:* "History or faith? We may never know. But the fact that Aksum could hold such a claim tells you how central it once was." — *Visual:* the chapel at night under stars.

---

### ACT V — Why it was erased (10:00–12:30)

**B41 [STILL]** — *VO:* "So what happened? How does a power ranked beside Rome slip out of the story?" — *Visual:* the great city, now quieter, dusk falling.

**B42 [STILL]** — *VO:* "From the seventh century, the world Aksum was built on began to change." — *Visual:* Red Sea trade winds shifting, ships turning away.

**B43 [STILL]** — *VO:* "The rise of new powers redrew the trade routes of the Red Sea. The harbors that made Aksum rich went quiet." — *Visual:* the port of Adulis emptying, sand creeping over stone.

**B44 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Overfarming and a drying climate wore down the land that fed the capital." — *Visual:* cracked, exhausted fields on the highland.

**B45 [STILL]** — *VO:* "The kings moved inland, and the great city slowly emptied — not conquered, but bypassed by history." — *Visual:* a procession leaving the city, stelae receding behind.

**B46 [STILL]** — *VO:* "And then came the deeper erasure. When Europeans later drew the map of 'civilization,' they wrote Africa out of it." — *Visual:* 19th-century European mapmakers, Africa left blank.

**B47 [STILL]** — *VO:* "An empire that minted gold, raised the tallest stones of the ancient world, and helped carry a world religion — was quietly left off the syllabus." — *Visual:* a history textbook, the page for Aksum missing.

**B48 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Not because the evidence was gone. The coins are in museums. The obelisks still stand. The churches still sing." — *Visual:* museum case of Aksumite coins, then the stelae, then a choir.

**B49 [CLIP]** — *VO:* "The evidence was never hidden. We were just never taught to look." — *Visual:* dawn breaking over the full stelae field, the empire 'remembered'.

---

### CLOSE (12:30–13:00)

**B50 [STILL]** — *VO:* "Aksum outlasted the Roman Empire in the West. It shaped trade, money, and faith across three continents. And it was African." — *Visual:* the king's silhouette over his city at golden hour.

**B51 [STILL]** — *VO:* "That's not a footnote. That's a chapter someone tore out." — *Visual:* the torn textbook page settling back into place, gold light.

**B52 [CARD]** — *VO:* "If this is the history you were never taught — subscribe. There is so much more they left out." — Outro card: OrigineX logo + Subscribe.
