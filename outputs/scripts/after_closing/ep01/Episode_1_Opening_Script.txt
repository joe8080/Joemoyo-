TITLE: AFTER THE LAST DANCE
EPISODE 1: "After Closing"
SCENE 01: THE CITY AND THE ROOM

TARGET DURATION: 120 - 150 seconds
VIBE: Tactile, atmospheric, slow-burn romantic. 35mm film grain, heavy contrast, amber and shadow.

[AUDIO - EXTERIOR]
The low, rhythmic rumble of an elevated train passing in the distance. The hiss of car tires on wet asphalt. A slow, mournful, solitary saxophone plays in the background, setting a moody jazz tone.

[VISUAL 01 - THE ESTABLISHING SHOT]
Cinematic 16:9. A rain-slicked Chicago street at night. A classic 1960s yellow cab cuts through the frame, its headlights reflecting in deep puddles. As the cab passes, it reveals a glowing, slightly buzzing neon sign extending over the pavement: "THE MARIGOLD - DANCING & SPIRITS."

[TEXT OVERLAY]
Bottom Left, crisp white serif font:
CHICAGO
AUTUMN, 1962

[VISUAL 02 - THE TRANSITION]
Slow push-in toward the heavy, brass-handled glass doors of The Marigold. The saxophone fades out, replaced by the muffled, muffled sound of a single piano playing a slow, searching melody. 

[VISUAL 03 - THE DETRITUS OF THE NIGHT]
Cut to the interior. The massive ballroom is empty of patrons. The main chandeliers are off; only the warm, amber practical lamps on the walls remain. 
Macro close-up: A half-empty highball glass sweating on a wooden table, next to a discarded, lipstick-stained cigarette in a brass ashtray. A white cloth wipes firmly across the frame, taking the glass and ashtray away. 

[VISUAL 04 - MARY JANE'S REALITY]
Medium shot. Mary Jane (27, white woman, muted sage dress, hair pinned up but a few strands falling loose from a long shift). She is meticulously wiping down the table. Her movements are tired but precise. She pauses, picking up a forgotten playbill, smoothing it out with her thumb, a quiet respect for the things people leave behind. 

[VISUAL 05 - THE SOURCE OF THE MUSIC]
Wide shot, looking across the sea of empty tables toward the distant stage. Stage-left, a single spotlight illuminates the grand piano. Isaiah (31, Black man, charcoal suit, tie loosened, jacket draped over the back of his chair) is playing. He isn't performing for a crowd; he is leaning into the keys, his eyes closed, playing for himself. 

[VISUAL 06 - THE RECOGNITION]
Medium close-up on Mary Jane. She stops wiping. The cloth hangs loose in her hand. She turns her head slowly toward the stage. The fatigue leaves her face, replaced by a quiet, absolute focus. She watches him not just as a coworker, but as an artist. 

[VISUAL 07 - THE HANDS]
Extreme close-up: Isaiah’s hands moving over the ivory keys. We see the repaired fraying on his left shirt cuff (a detail she fixed). The music swells—a complex, beautiful, original phrase. 

[VISUAL 08 - THE SHARED SILENCE]
Over-the-shoulder shot from behind the piano. Isaiah opens his eyes, staring out into the dark, empty ballroom. He doesn't stop playing, but his gaze finds Mary Jane standing perfectly still by the tables. 
Cut to Mary Jane. She knows he's looking at her. She doesn't look away. A faint, knowing smile touches the corner of her mouth. The chemistry in this silent look is heavy, anchoring the entire room.

[VISUAL 09 - THE MISTAKE]
Medium shot on Isaiah. He holds her gaze, perhaps distracted by it, and his fingers reach for a progression. He hits a jarring, dissonant chord. 
[AUDIO] The beautiful music stops dead. 

[VISUAL 10 - THE REACTION]
Isaiah drops his head, running a hand over his face in exhaustion. Across the room, Mary Jane's smile turns into a look of fond amusement. She picks up her tray of glasses and begins walking slowly toward the stage, her footsteps echoing lightly on the hardwood. 

[VISUAL 11 - THE APPROACH]
She sets the tray down on the table closest to the piano. She doesn't look at him immediately, occupying herself with straightening a chair, letting the silence stretch just a beat longer. Finally, she speaks, her voice soft but echoing in the massive room.

MARY JANE (V.O.)
"You always stop there."

[VISUAL 12 - THE CONNECTION]
Isaiah lifts his head. The frustration is gone. He leans an elbow on the piano, looking at her with a slow, devastating charm.

ISAIAH (V.O.)
"You always listen?"

MARY JANE (V.O.)
(A quiet laugh)
"Hard not to. Same mistake, six weeks."
