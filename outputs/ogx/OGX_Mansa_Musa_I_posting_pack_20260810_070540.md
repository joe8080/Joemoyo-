# 1. THUMBNAIL BRIEF

### THE CALL — DOMINANT SUBJECT

**Hero object: the Catalan Atlas enthroned figure, BnF Ms. Espagnol 30, sheet 6 — archival scan, public domain (Gallica).**

The locked spec prefers a human face with direct eye contact. It cannot have one here, and the reason is the episode's thesis. `people/mansa-musa` (verified) carries no likeness, and **no authenticated likeness of Mansa Musa exists**. He is a real historical person, so the house rule bars an AI face outright — and here a generated face would be a fabrication twice over: an invented likeness of a man whose erasure is the subject of the film.

The Atlas figure is the correct substitution because it is not a portrait — it is a *document*, and it is the single hero object the whole episode argues about. It also happens to carry a drawn face with near-frontal gaze, which recovers most of the eye-contact lever without inventing anything. It must be captioned as a document, on the thumbnail and in the film.

Amber is the wealth/empire pop, and this episode's spine is a **state and its bullion**, not a battlefield.

**The one risk, and its control:** amber #FFF3CD sits very close to gold #C9A84C and to the white hook. Uncontrolled, it muddies all three. If the art pass shows the gold label losing separation, drop the amber to 15% before touching any other value.

The red date badge is fixed brand furniture under lever 4. It does not count as a second pop and must not be recoloured to match the amber.

### ART-PASS CHECKLIST

**□ ARCHIVAL-VS-AI CALL — ARCHIVAL, MANDATORY. NO EXCEPTIONS.**
Mansa Musa is a real person. House rule: real people get archival images, never AI-generated faces. Compounding it, `people/mansa-musa` (verified) records no likeness — none survives. The permitted asset is the BnF Ms. Espagnol 30 scan, composited and relit, never redrawn. AI is permitted for: the near-black ground, grain, dust, and the light falloff. **AI is forbidden for: the figure, the face, the crown, the hands, the throne, and any lettering that reads as period script.**

**□ CAPTION OBLIGATION.** The Atlas figure carries a caption wherever it appears in the film — *"Catalan Atlas, 1375 — a European artist's imagining. No authenticated likeness of Mansa Musa exists."* The thumbnail carries the 1375 badge as its minimum equivalent. It must never be presented as a portrait.

**□ ANACHRONISM — THE HAND OBJECT. DB CONFLICT, DO NOT RESOLVE IN ART.**
`events/…catalan-atlas…` (conf. 60) says "an orb of gold and a golden sceptre." `oral_evidence` (Catalan Atlas, reliability high, corr. 3) says "a gold nugget." Two records, two objects. **Composite the scan as-is and let the artefact answer** — crop or shadow the hand rather than reconstruct it, and never generate a replacement object. Flagged for a human to settle against sheet 6 before the film locks.

**□ ANACHRONISM — THE CROWN. Keep the European crown. Do not "correct" it.**
The Atlas gives him a European-style crown because a Majorcan Christian workshop drew him. That is the evidence, and correcting it to West African regalia would falsify the one document the episode rests on.

**□ ANACHRONISM — SCRIPT.** No AI-generated Arabic or Catalan lettering anywhere in frame. Garbled script is the single most exposing tell in this genre and it would discredit an evidence-first channel instantly. Any period text must be lifted from the scan or omitted.

**□ ANACHRONISM — PROPS.** No coins (Mali's trade ran on gold dust and salt bars per `civilizations/trans-saharan-gold-salt-network`, conf. 74 — minted coinage in frame is a tell). No modern camel tack. No Djinguereber mosque in the same frame as the 1375 badge — the mosque dates to c.1327 per `places/timbuktu-mali` (conf. 75) but its photographable fabric is heavily re-rendered, and pairing it with a 1375 badge invites a false read. No scales, no bullion bars, no treasure chest.

**□ LOGO RESERVE PROTECTED.** Bottom-right 300 × 160 px empty. Check three things at export: the amber halo does not bleed into it, the hero figure's throne base does not encroach, and the hook's descenders and drop shadow stop clear of it. The hook sits in the upper two-thirds of the text column precisely so the stamp never lands on a key word.

**□ MOBILE LEGIBILITY.** Test at 210 px wide. "MAPPED, THEN ERASED" must hold at that size; the gold label may go soft, which is acceptable — it is the second tier, not the hook.

### RANKING

**1st — T1. WINNER.**
It is the only one of the three that is the film's actual thesis rather than a correction to someone else's. Two concrete numbers front-loaded (1375, 150 years), the head term "Mansa Musa" inside the first 30 characters for search, and a reversal that lands without needing the viewer to already hold a belief: *they had it, and they had it early*. It also owns the least-competed keyword in the set — "catalan atlas mansa musa" is mid-tail and effectively unserved on YouTube, while "mansa musa" alone is saturated with net-worth content this title does not have to fight. The identity/suppression angle is carried by the title itself, not bolted on. Its one weakness is that "150 Years Before It Got There" asks the viewer to hold two dates at once; the thumbnail badge (1375) and hook (MAPPED, THEN ERASED) resolve that in a glance, which is why the pair must ship together.

**2nd — T3. A/B ALTERNATE.**
Highest raw cold-CTR of the three, because it collides head-on with something the audience already believes and names the number they came looking for. It converts debunk-intent search ("mansa musa net worth") into honest traffic. It ranks second rather than first for two reasons: it makes the film *about* a myth rather than about an empire, which is exactly the reduction Chapter Five argues against — and the $400bn term will pull an audience whose retention on a 14-minute constitutional-history film is unproven. Run it as the documented A/B against T1; if it wins CTR but loses AVD, T1 is still the correct long-run title.

**3rd — T2. Hold in reserve.**
Evidentially the strongest sentence in the whole pack — al-Umari's gold-price claim is the best-corroborated item in the dossier (`oral_evidence`, reliability high, corroboration 4). But as a title it underperforms: "held down" is an abstraction where "mapped" and "$400 billion" are concrete, and the payoff is a negation. A title whose reward is *"the exciting thing you heard didn't happen"* reads as a correction, not a story. Keep the sentence — it earns its place in the description and the pinned comment, where the evidence context makes it land.

> In 1375, a Jewish cartographer in Majorca drew the emperor of Mali at the centre of the medieval world — crowned, enthroned, gold in his hand — working from intelligence carried by traders across the Sahara.
