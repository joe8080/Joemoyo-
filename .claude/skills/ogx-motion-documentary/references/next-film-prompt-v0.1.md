# OGX production prompt — next-film test v0.1 (from MJ/GPT, 21 Oct 2026)
This is the source prompt for the gated workflow in SKILL.md. Kept verbatim for reference; the first test film is Isandlwana (proposed). Improve it after each film from the learning log.

Develop Isandlwana: How the Zulu Army Defeated Britain, targeting approximately 9–12 minutes, only where the evidence supports that length.

First check existing work. Read our current Eden board, episode research and OGX standards. Reuse any relevant preparation. Preserve existing releases and Adwa files.

Creative direction: retain Adwa's warm gold landscapes, archive textures, dark backgrounds and clear terrain maps. Combine these with the storytelling Joe liked in the longer Benin film. Keep the established narrator and verified voice settings.

Viewer promise: explain how the Zulu army won this battle. Give Zulu decisions and organisation proper attention; avoid making the entire story a list of British mistakes.

Before recording: produce the source-linked claim ledger, clean narration, pronunciation list and scene plan for MJ's review. Check dates, chronology, troop and casualty estimates, disputed explanations and the distinction between winning the battle and winning the war. Include African perspectives and identify limitations in colonial accounts. Remove unsupported certainty.

Opening: establish the battlefield, stakes and central question immediately. Show meaningful evidence within 30 seconds and begin answering within the first minute. No lengthy introductory geography lesson.

Prove the visuals: first build a 45–60-second representative sequence combining narration, a readable map, authentic evidence and an illustrative action shot. Action must involve movement within the scene—people, equipment, smoke or terrain interaction. Camera zooms alone do not satisfy this requirement.

Visual accuracy: verify clothing, weapons, landscape and formations. Label reconstructions. Do not use film scenes as historical evidence or copy their compositions shot for shot. Maps must distinguish documented movements from approximate interpretations.

Phone readability: one main point per screen. Test essential labels and captions at phone size. Keep source details in the accompanying ledger and description, with readable short credits where appropriate.

Production: retain separate narration, music, effects and caption tracks, plus the editable project. Use only existing spending approvals; report any additional cost before incurring it.

Delivery: provide the review MP4, SRT captions, editable project, voiced script, claim and asset ledgers, change log, three title options, two thumbnail concepts and one self-contained Short. Verify that shared links open and the actual video is present.

QC: check the complete export for picture/audio duration, clipped words, pronunciation, music balance, caption timing and ending. State precisely what was inspected. Keep "rendered", "QC passed" and "Joe approved" separate.

Learning: record factual corrections needed after recording, visual rework, actual cost and missing deliverables. Use those results to improve this prompt after the film. Do not publish or schedule without Joe's approval.
