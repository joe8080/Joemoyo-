## 1. Research summary (what the evidence allows us to say)
1. **Kongo was an organised kingdom before the Portuguese arrived.**
   - Its capital, Mbanza Kongo, stood in what is now northern Angola.
   - It had provinces, royal officials, taxation and currency (nzimbu shells, raffia cloth), and regional trade in copper, salt and cloth.
   - *Status: secondary, amber until checked against Thornton 2020 or Hilton 1985 (gap G2).*
2. **The relationship began in the 1480s and turned religious and diplomatic quickly.**
   - The 1491 baptism of King Nzinga a Nkuwu.
   - Afonso's reign from about 1506.
   - Schools in the kingdom, described in 1516.
   - His son Henrique named a bishop by Leo X in 1518.
   - Embassies and an act of obedience to the pope.
3. **Kongo paid for this partly in people, and Afonso took part.**
   - Manuel's 1512 instructions ask that the ships come back loaded "with slaves, copper and ivory".
   - In 1514, Afonso describes sending more than 400 war captives to load a Portuguese ship.
4. **The 1526 letters object to two things:**
   - Portuguese merchants trading banned goods freely, which let vassals grow rich and rebel against royal authority;
   - the kidnapping and sale of free people, including nobles and the king's relatives.
5. **In July 1526 Afonso asked for no trade in slaves at all.** By **October 1526** he had set up a **licensing law** instead: three named court officials had to check every purchase, to tell free people from those lawfully enslaved. **That's regulation and royal control, not abolition.**
6. **Portugal answered** (João III, end of 1529):
   - doubting that Kongo was being depopulated;
   - proposing a supervised market;
   - saying that 10,000 slaves a year would honour Kongo more than "no trade";
   - and, in the same letter, telling Portuguese priests how to buy slaves for their keep.

### Limits
- We have **only the kings' side**: letters written for royal readers, each making a case. The voices of the people who were seized aren't in these sources.
- The letters were **written by secretaries** (Dom João Teixeira is named in both 1526 letters) and signed by Afonso. They aren't "in his own hand".
- Brásio's headings modernise. For example, he heads the 1514 letter "S. Salvador do Congo", but the capital's renaming as São Salvador comes later. **Don't use "São Salvador" for Afonso's time.**
- We have **no portrait of Afonso** made from life. Later European engravings are imagined. No AI face of Afonso, of any named person, or of anyone else.

## 3. What the 1526 letters actually say (key passages, working translation)
**L3, 6 July 1526**
- (468) A ship "of Your Highness's trade" reached "our port of Soyo" (Sonho) on 26 June. Kongo was short of wine and flour for the Mass. Afonso sends condolences on the death of Queen Leonor, "our sister".
- (470) "Sir, Your Highness should know how our kingdom is being lost in so many ways…"
  - He blames the freedom your factors and officials give merchants to settle in this kingdom with shops and with goods that "we have forbidden".
  - These goods spread so abundantly "that many vassals who were in our obedience rise up against it, because they have these things in greater abundance than we do".
- (470) "Each day the said merchants take our people (naturaes), sons of the land and sons of our nobles and vassals, and our relatives."
  - "Thieves and men of bad conscience steal them, wishing to have the goods of that kingdom", and bring them to sell.
  - "…so great is this corruption and licentiousness that our land is being entirely depopulated."
- (470–471) "We need from those kingdoms nothing more than priests and a few people to teach in the schools, and no goods, only wine and flour for the holy Sacrament…"
  - "…because it is our will that in these kingdoms there should be no trade in slaves nor outlet for them."
  - Written by "dom Joham Teyxeira". Signed "ElRey dom A.°".

**L6, 18 October 1526**
- (488–489) He asks for **two physicians, two apothecaries and a surgeon**, with medicines. He says most people heal "with herbs and sticks and other ways of their ancient custom".
- (489) "Many of our people, from the great desire they have for the goods… steal many of our people who are free and exempt (forros e isentos)."
  - "Very often they steal nobles and the sons of nobles, and our relatives, and take them to sell to the white men who are in our kingdoms."
  - They bring them "hidden, and others by night, so as not to be recognised".
- (490) "As soon as they are in the power of the said white men, they are branded and marked with fire."
  - Royal guards find them at embarkation. The buyers say they bought them "and cannot say from whom". So the king "must do justice and restore the free to their liberty".
- (490) **The law:**
  - Any white man in the kingdoms who buys "peças" (pieces, i.e. people) in any way must first notify **three nobles and officials of our court**: **Dom Pedro Manipunzo, Dom Manuel Manissaba, our chief meirinho [bailiff], and Gonçalo Pires, our chief armador [outfitter]**. They will see whether each person "is captive or free".
  - Once cleared, there will be no obstacle and they may be embarked. Anyone who does otherwise loses them.
  - Afonso grants "this favour and latitude… for the share in it that belongs to Your Highness".
  - Written by Dom João Teixeira. Signed by Afonso.

**L7, João III, end of 1529**
- (524) "You say in your letters that you do not want there to be a trade in slaves in your kingdom, because your land is being depopulated." João III believes Afonso says this because of quarrels with the Portuguese.
  - João III adds that he's told Kongo is so populous "that it seems no slave ever left it", and that Afonso has slaves bought from outside and marries them and makes them Christians.
- (525) He proposes a **designated place in the market** where two of Afonso's servants would check whether those sold were natives or from outside, with no sale in houses unless the two were present.
- (525–527) "I say now that I am willing, as you say, that there be no trade in slaves in your kingdom." He'd send only flour and wine for Masses, one small caravel a year.
  - "But to me it does not seem an honour to you nor to your kingdom, for it is more praiseworthy that **10,000 slaves**, and ten thousand manillas, and as many ivory tusks, are taken from Kongo each year, than that it be said there is no trade in Kongo and only one ship a year goes." (Brásio's footnote reads "x" as 10,000.)
- (536–537) In his rules for the priests he sends to Kongo, João III tells them to pool their money and ask the king "for men to go and buy slaves for you", and to keep their trade "with as much quiet and honesty as you can".
