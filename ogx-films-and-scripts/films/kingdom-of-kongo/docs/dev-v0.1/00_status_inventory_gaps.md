## 3. Proposed story (one paragraph)
We open on the real 6 July 1526 page and Afonso's line that his kingdom "is being lost". Within the first minute, we give the honest answer: he wasn't asking Portugal to end slavery. He was trying to stop free Kongolese, including nobles and his own relatives, being kidnapped and sold, and to win back control of a trade that was arming his rivals with foreign goods. Then we step back and build Kongo's world: the hilltop capital, the provinces, the money, the elected kingship. Only then do we tell the relationship: 1483, the 1491 baptism, Afonso's schools, the bishop son, and Kongo paying in copper, ivory and people (his own 1514 shipment of war captives). The turn is the October letter. Three months after asking for "no trade in slaves", he passes a law that every person bought must be checked by three named officials. That's regulation, not abolition. João III's reply (end of 1529) shows Lisbon's view: better 10,000 slaves a year than "no trade". We close by answering the opening question and stating plainly what the letters can't tell us.
