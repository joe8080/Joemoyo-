## 1. Story treatment: "One day, one hill"
**Question:** how did a Zulu army get within a few miles of a British camp without its main body being found, and then destroy it in an afternoon?

**Spine:**
1. **Open on the discovery** (around midday on 22 January): a British patrol looks down into the Ngwebeni valley and finds the whole army.
2. **Pull back:** the scale of what happened that day, and how London told it (*The Graphic*, 1879).
3. **The army itself:**
   - amabutho;
   - weapons;
   - the horns of the buffalo, explicitly as doctrine, not a record.
4. **Why it was there:**
   - Frere's ultimatum;
   - the invasion;
   - the army sets out in mid-January.
5. **The approach:**
   - Chelmsford's camp;
   - the commanders bring the regiments together unseen;
   - before dawn, Chelmsford splits his force;
   - the moon accounts, attributed and debated.
6. **Back to midday. The battle, as one movement:**
   - the regiments rise;
   - the horns open;
   - the line holds, then Mkhosana, by Zulu accounts;
   - both horns close;
   - the road home is cut;
   - the line breaks;
   - the camp is overrun;
   - the eclipse.
7. **The answer at the climax.** How the Zulu army won, said once, where it is earned.
8. **What it cost and how we know:**
   - the dead on both sides;
   - British records versus Zulu accounts collected later.
9. **Battle, not war:** Rorke's Drift, Ulundi, the capture, the division of Zululand.
10. **Memory:** the 1999 isiqu memorial, then the hill today.

**What stays OGX:**
- Joe's voice, type and colour;
- reconstruction labels and credit strips;
- readable real-ground maps;
- −14 LUFS;
- the sign-off.

**What's different from Adwa:**
- in-the-action opening;
- no question card;
- no "This is the story of…" line;
- no numbered acts (date and time markers instead);
- one continuous battle arc;
- the answer at the climax, not a recap;
- ending on the memorial and the land;
- the badge at a lull;
- three-movement score.

**Zulu perspective:**
- The Zulu command's actions drive the turning points: the concealed approach, the rising, the horns, the road cut.
- Wording separates **documented actions** (the army assembled unseen in the valley; the regiments rose; the right horn cut the road) from **interpretations of intention** (the moon accounts, which stay attributed and debated). Nothing new about Zulu intentions is added.
