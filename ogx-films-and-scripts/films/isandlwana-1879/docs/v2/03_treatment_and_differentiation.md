# Isandlwana v2: story treatment and creative differentiation note
Prepared by Claude, 22 October 2026. **Private. For MJ and Joe.**

## Logline
One day in January 1879, told from the valley where the Zulu army waited, in the words of the men who fought it.

## The spine (what changes from v1.x)
v1.x asked the British question: "what went wrong?". Then it answered "how did the Zulu win?" in a summary at the end.

v2 lives **inside the Zulu army's day**. We start in the Ngwebeni valley before noon, with regiments sitting in the grass and not meaning to fight, because the moon was "wrong". We end at dusk, when the army walks back to the Ngutu hills carrying captured guns, and a British officer sketches the silent field.

The "how did they win" answer isn't a lecture at the end. **It's built into the scenes:**
- the slow march;
- the hidden valley;
- the regiments that rose without orders;
- the induna who got them up again;
- the horn that went behind the hill.

The witnesses are real and named by role: an Umbonambi veteran, a Nokenke veteran, Uguku of the Kandampemvu, and a Nokenke prisoner. **Their words appear on screen as text, attributed to source and page.** The narrator never puts words in their mouths.

## Creative differentiation note (Adwa is the benchmark, not the template)
| | Adwa v1.5 | Isandlwana v2 |
|---|---|---|
| **Point of view** | Wide and strategic: two empires, three Italian brigades | **One army's day, at ground level.** The narrator walks with the regiments |
| **Signature device** | Brigade maps; Menelik's diplomacy | **"In their words":** Zulu testimony cards, attributed, with word-by-word reveals. They're silent on screen, so the words carry the moment |
| **Terrain** | Mountain plates | **3D terrain flights** over the real battlefield model: Ngwebeni to the iNyoni heights to the hill. The map turns into the ground |
| **Colour** | Warm epic throughout | **Time-of-day script:** blue morning, then hard midday, then smoke-brown battle, then eclipse-dark, then dusk |
| **Sound** | Score only | **Score plus a sound-design layer:** wind on the plateau; volleys "tumbling one upon another"; the 7-pounders; the rush through the grass; near-silence under the eclipse |
| **Archive** | European engravings and photographs | **Zulu commanders on screen** (Ntshingwayo, identified as; Dabulamanzi), Fripp's 1879 field sketch, and Crealock's eyewitness sketch from the evening of the battle |
| **Ending** | Diplomatic aftermath | **The army walking home** with captured guns, then the price: Ulundi, exile, partition |

## Guardrails held
- **No AI faces of named real people.** The induna, the commanders and Mehlokazulu appear in archive photos or as unnamed figures. The induna's figure is shown as "an induna", and his name comes with a label.
- **No invented speech.** The shouts ("Usutu!") appear only as quoted text.
- **No flags, insignia or fake documents.**
- **No compositions from *Zulu* (1964) or *Zulu Dawn* (1979).** No shot echoes their framing: the long red line from behind the officers, or the impi massed on the skyline above Rorke's Drift.
- Avoid "never colonised" and "first African victory".
- Contested points stay framed: the army's size (14,000 or 25,000), times, the induna's name, Zulu losses.
