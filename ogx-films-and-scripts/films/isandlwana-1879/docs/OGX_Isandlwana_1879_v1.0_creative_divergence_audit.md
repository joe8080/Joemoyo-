## Verdict
Isandlwana v1.0 is **a disguised reskin of Adwa in its frame**. The opening, question card, title, numbered acts, consequences act, recap ending, sign-off, subscribe timing and music pattern all follow Adwa's order, often line for line. The middle (02–05) is where it's genuinely its own film: the hidden army, the horns, and the road cut on real satellite ground.

The fix is mainly **re-ordering and cutting existing material**, not re-generating it.

## 2. Creative weaknesses in v1.0
1. **The opening isn't Isandlwana's own.**
   - The strongest thing this battle has is a moment: the British patrol looking down into the Ngwebeni valley and finding the whole army there. In v1.0 that's buried at **I 2:59**.
   - The film instead opens on a London print with Adwa's sentence.
2. **The question card and title are a template.** At I 0:34–0:52 they repeat Adwa beat for beat. A returning viewer will feel it.
3. **Numbered acts fight the story.**
   - Isandlwana is mostly one day. Seven numbered parchment acts break the momentum, with a header at every step: 2:59 FOUND, 3:21 THE HORNS CLOSE.
   - The battle is cut into three pieces with three music resets.
4. **The battle has no single arc.** It runs from discovery (I 2:59), through the charge (I 3:33–3:41), to the road cut (I 3:56) and the collapse (I 4:03). It's six map shots, each with legend and tag, alternating with AI shots, at the same 5–6 s rhythm as Adwa's slower political acts.
5. **The answer comes in an Adwa-style recap** (I 5:40–6:05) instead of being shown at the climax. By the time the film explains how they won, the battle is two minutes behind us.
6. **Zulu perspective is said, not felt.** The source caveat ("Zulu accounts were often collected later…") is one line at I 4:38. The structure is still built around British decisions: Frere, Chelmsford's split, Pulleine. Ntshingwayo's decision to keep the army hidden gets one line (I 2:17).
7. **Archive is under-used as evidence.**
   - The Holden 1879 sketch (I 4:38) is a real British field sketch, but it's shown only as a backdrop.
   - *The Graphic* print does its best work as evidence of how Britain told the story, not as a hook.
8. **The subscribe badge at 2:00** sits on the commanders shot (Z07), which is a story beat, not a lull.

**Working, so keep it:**
- the hidden-army sequence (I 2:17–2:59);
- the doctrine diagram;
- the satellite battle maps' readability and labels;
- the road-cut beat (I 3:56);
- the eclipse kept still and framed honestly (I 4:15);
- all contested-claim framing;
- the voice, captions, credits, AI labels and loudness.

## 3. Revised storytelling and visual treatment: "One day, one hill"
**Spine:**
- Most of the film is the 22nd of January, told around the clock: before dawn, mid-morning, around midday, early afternoon, that night.
- The context (the ultimatum, the amabutho, the march) is folded in as quick flashbacks, not numbered acts.
- **The hill of Isandlwana is the recurring frame.** We return to the same view as the day changes: storm (Z01), smoke (Z23), the dimmed sun (Z25), dusk (Z02), and today (I08, I09).

**Point of view:**
- The Zulu command's decisions drive the story:
  - stay hidden in the valley;
  - the regiments rise;
  - the horns run wide;
  - the road is cut.
- British mistakes come in as what the Zulu command exploited, not as the main thread.

**Visual grammar (different from Adwa):**
1. **No numbered parchment act cards.** Chapters become **time-of-day markers on the terrain**, e.g. "BEFORE DAWN · 22 JANUARY", "AROUND MIDDAY", "EARLY AFTERNOON". They use the same OGX type, but a new device. Only approximate times that are already in the ledger.
2. **One accumulating battle map.** Use one satellite frame of the ground: the camp, the hill, the Ngwebeni and the road to Rorke's Drift.
   - Each return adds the next movement, and earlier moves stay faintly visible.
   - It cuts against grass-level reconstructions: map → ground → map.
   - The legend and tags stay, but appear once per return, not once per shot.
3. **Archive as evidence, not wallpaper.**
   - *The Graphic* moves to the "how Britain told it" passage, beside "The usual story…" and the source caveat.
   - The Holden sketch gets a held detail push with its date and credit: "a British sketch, 1879".
4. **Rhythm:**
   - slower, longer holds in the hidden-valley build (7–10 s);
   - then a hard acceleration from "The whole Zulu army was there" to "it broke" (2–4 s shots);
   - then stillness after the camp falls (the eclipse still, the cairns, the memorial).
5. **Sound in three movements instead of one cue per act:**
   - a low, sparse bed for the build;
   - one continuous battle cue from discovery to collapse, with no act resets;
   - near-silence at the eclipse, then a lament for the cost and the memorial.

   All of this can be cut from the **existing 9 cues** (C00–C08). No new music.
6. **Ending:**
   - land on the isiqu memorial and the hill at dusk;
   - deliver "how they won" at the climax, right after the road is cut and the line breaks;
   - no recap triad;
   - keep the channel signature "This has been OrigineX Human Archives."
