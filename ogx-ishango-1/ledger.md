### Debated (CONTESTED readings; always attributed in the narration)
- L05 — "Is it a tally? A game? A calendar? Or marks we can't read at all?" — D01, D03, D04, D06 (attributed reading)
- L18 — "And the quartz? De Heinzelin suggested engraving or tattooing. The museum's brochure mentions the ritual scarification of skin. These are only hypotheses." — D07 (attributed reading)
- L26 — "Jean de Heinzelin admitted the patterns might be chance. But in 1962 he wrote that they seemed deliberately planned, perhaps as an arithmetical game." — D01 (attributed reading)
- L27 — "He saw doubling in the middle column, and the museum points to the same pairs. Three and six. Four and eight. Five and ten." — D01, C10 (attributed reading)
- L28 — "He also noted that eleven, thirteen, seventeen and nineteen are prime numbers." — D01, D02 (attributed reading)
- L29 — "Not everyone agrees. Pletser and Huylebrouck rejected the prime-number idea, and the museum says there isn't enough proof." — D02 (attributed reading)
- L30 — "Their own conclusion, which they call unspectacular: probably a tool for counting events or things, kept by someone who mixed bases ten and twelve." — D03 (attributed reading)
- L31 — "Another reading, from the researcher Marshack, linked the marks to a lunar calendar." — D04 (attributed reading)
- L32 — "Keller calls that idea now largely discredited." — D04 (attributed reading)
- L33 — "The Smithsonian calls them tally marks, and says they may have been used to add or multiply." — D05, C13 (attributed reading)
- L34 — "Keller is the most sceptical. Some marks are worn or barely visible, he says, and that makes any numeric reading suspect." — D06 (attributed reading)
- L35 — "A notch, he writes, may be nothing more than a mark." — D06 (attributed reading)
- L36 — "Even de Heinzelin only said he suspected the marks were more than pure decoration." — D08 (attributed reading)
- L44 — "And how much can they tell us? Less than we'd like. Counting, a game, a calendar, or simply marks: each reading has its defenders, and none is proven." — D01, D06, D08 (attributed reading)
