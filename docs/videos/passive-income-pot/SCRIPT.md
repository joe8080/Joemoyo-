# Film 2: "How Big a Pot Do You Need for £1,000 a Month?"

Target 5:00 (about 700 words at 140 wpm). Education only, not personal advice. Figures are illustrations, not forecasts.
Voice: Joe's cloned voice (see VOICE.md). Cold open is Joe's own beat, same as Film 1.

## Cold open (0:00–0:20)
What would it take to get £1,000 landing in your account every month, without working for it? Not a dream number. An actual number. Let's work it out, step by step.

## 1. The target (0:20–1:00)
£1,000 a month is £12,000 a year. That's the target. Everything else is just one question: how big a pot throws off £12,000 a year?

## 2. The yield rule (1:00–2:00)
The answer depends on yield: the share of the pot you take out each year. At 3%, you need £400,000. At 4%, you need £300,000. At 5%, you need £240,000. A higher yield means a smaller pot, but it also means more risk to your income. Chasing yield is how people get burned. So the honest range is the wide one: roughly £240,000 to £400,000.

## 3. How long it takes (2:00–3:20)
Now compounding. Suppose someone invests £500 a month. At an illustrative 7% a year, that's about £86,500 after ten years, from £60,000 paid in. After twenty years, about £260,000, from £120,000 paid in. Over half of that is growth they never had to work for. At 5% a year, twenty years gives about £205,500. Same effort, a different return: that gap is why costs and patience matter more than clever picks.

## 4. Where it lives (3:20–4:10)
In the UK, the wrapper matters. A Stocks & Shares ISA lets growth and income stay free of tax, up to the yearly allowance, currently £20,000. Many people prefer low-cost UCITS funds inside it, since they're built for UK investors. Fewer moving parts, fewer fees, fewer chances to tinker.

## 5. The honest part (4:10–4:40)
None of this is guaranteed. Markets fall. Returns arrive unevenly. A pot that looks big enough on paper can still fall short in a bad decade. That's why people check the plan once or twice a year, not every day.

## Close (4:40–5:00)
So: £12,000 a year, a yield you can live with, and time. Work out your own numbers, and don't take this as advice. If this helped, subscribe, and I'll see you in the next one.
